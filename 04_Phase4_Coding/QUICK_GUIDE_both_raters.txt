QUICK GUIDE - one page (same rules as the Instructions tab, just shorter)
========================================================================
Both raters (Kunal and Leah) use this identical guide.

FOR EACH PAPER
--------------
1. Open the PDF named in the pdf_file column.
2. Find the Methods section (it may be called "Methods", "Experiments" or "Experimental setup").
3. Answer the 5 yellow dropdowns below, in order. Use Ctrl+F with the search words given.
4. Paste into evidence_quote the ONE sentence your first answer (split_unit) is based on.
5. Save. About 10 minutes per paper. If you're stuck on a column for more than a minute, pick "unclear".


THE 5 COLUMNS
-------------

1) split_unit - Were whole PEOPLE kept on one side of the train/test split, or were PIECES shuffled?
   Search: subject  participant  leave-one  LOSO  split  fold  segment  epoch  sample
   - The paper says PEOPLE were divided (e.g. "leave-one-subject-out",
     "subjects were assigned to training or test")                              -> subject
   - The paper cut recordings into pieces (segments / epochs / windows / samples)
     and then split or shuffled THE PIECES, with no mention of people            -> epoch
   - You can't tell (e.g. just "10-fold cross-validation was used")             -> unclear

2) connectivity_scope - Were the connection numbers calculated from each piece or person on its own?
   Search: connectivity  adjacency  matrix  PLV  PLI  coherence  correlation  average  group
   - Each matrix is calculated from one piece or one person's own data          -> within-fold
   - Numbers averaged or pooled ACROSS people, or one matrix built from everyone -> whole-dataset
   - The paper doesn't say                                                      -> unclear

3) threshold_fit - Did they cut out weak connections?
   Search: threshold  strongest  top  sparsity  binar  retain  keep  %
   - No cutting at all                                                          -> none
   - A fixed cut applied to each matrix on its own ("keep the strongest 30%"),
     OR the paper says the cut-off was chosen on training data only             -> within-fold
   - They tried several cut-offs and picked the best on all the data            -> whole-dataset
   - They cut, but you can't tell how the cut-off was chosen                    -> unclear

4) normalization_fit - Did they rescale the numbers?  (IGNORE "batch normalization" completely)
   Search: normaliz  standardiz  z-score  scal  min-max
   - No rescaling mentioned                                                     -> none
   - Each piece rescaled using only its own values, OR scaling fitted on the
     training set only                                                          -> within-fold
   - The whole dataset rescaled together before splitting                       -> whole-dataset
   - "The features were normalized." and nothing more                           -> unclear

5) selection_fit - Did they pick only some channels or frequency bands?
   Search: select  best  optimal  channel  electrode  band  significant  p <
   - Used everything, or chose in advance for a stated reason                   -> none
   - Paper says the choosing used training data only                            -> within-fold
   - They compared bands/channels and the BEST one became the main model, or
     picked features by a statistical test on all the data                      -> whole-dataset
     (Just reporting a result for each band separately is NOT selection.)
   - They selected something but you can't tell how                             -> unclear


IF THE PAPER TESTS ITS MODEL MORE THAN ONE WAY
-----------------------------------------------
Answer for the way behind the accuracy number in the ABSTRACT. Write the other way(s) in notes.
If the abstract gives NO accuracy number: answer for the way used in the paper's MAIN results table,
and write the other way(s) in notes. (Provisional rule, same for both raters.)


WORKED EXAMPLE (an invented paper, not one of ours)
---------------------------------------------------
  "Resting EEG was recorded from 40 patients and 40 healthy volunteers. Each recording was cut into
   2-second segments. For each segment we computed the phase-locking value between every pair of
   electrodes, and kept the strongest 20% of connections in each matrix. All features were
   standardized using the mean and standard deviation of the entire dataset. The segments were
   randomly split into 80% for training and 20% for testing. The gamma band gave the highest
   accuracy, so it was used for the final model."

  split_unit          epoch          the PIECES (segments) were split, no mention of people
  connectivity_scope  within-fold    computed "for each segment"
  threshold_fit       within-fold    a fixed 20% cut applied to each matrix on its own
  normalization_fit   whole-dataset  scaled with statistics of the ENTIRE dataset
  selection_fit       whole-dataset  best band chosen by accuracy became the final model
  evidence_quote      The segments were randomly split into 80% for training and 20% for testing.


THE THREE RULES
---------------
1. Work alone. Don't discuss any specific paper with the other rater until you've both finished.
2. Only what the paper says. No guessing, no looking at their code, no emailing authors.
3. "Unclear" is a real answer. Most papers don't say. That's what we're measuring.

Questions about what a column MEANS are fine to ask. Questions about a specific paper are not.
